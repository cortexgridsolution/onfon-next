# Onfon Next Control Station — Visual Direction

## Stylistic Approaches

### Theme Name: Quiet Operations
Very light neo-Swiss operations software with editorial typography, deep ink, cobalt actions, and small coral risk signals. It treats the control station as a calm instrument panel rather than a conventional admin template.

**Probability:** 0.06

### Theme Name: Field Signal
A more tactile dealer-network direction with warm paper, stamped labels, muted green, and map-like data marks. It would feel grounded in the human network behind every financed accessory.

**Probability:** 0.03

### Theme Name: Night Shift Console
A dark, high-contrast operations room with electric blue and amber status lights, optimized for long monitoring sessions. It is deliberately more cinematic but less aligned to the supplied airy reference.

**Probability:** 0.08

## Selected Direction: Quiet Operations

### Design Movement
Neo-Swiss editorial utility: the restraint and information hierarchy of Swiss International Style, translated into a modern fintech operations interface with soft material depth and precise data rhythm.

### Core Principles
1. **Calm authority:** generous off-white space, crisp ink typography, and restrained borders make complex decisions feel understandable.
2. **Signal over decoration:** cobalt is reserved for actions and active states; coral is reserved for risk, exceptions, and attention.
3. **Asymmetric structure:** landing sections use offset copy and floating interface artifacts; app pages use persistent navigation and detail drawers rather than centered cards.
4. **Evidence in the interface:** every CTA and metric is paired with a reason, timestamp, status, or next step.

### Color Philosophy
The foundation is a warm mineral white rather than pure white so the UI feels like a physical operations desk. Deep ink gives the product seriousness and high legibility. A single ownable cobalt, **Onfon Blue**, becomes the visual shorthand for a permitted action or healthy flow. A dry coral is used sparingly for suppressed customers, rejected inventory, and review states; it should create attention without making the whole product feel anxious. Pale blue and pale sand surfaces provide grouping without relying on heavy shadows.

### Layout Paradigm
Use a left-weighted editorial composition: short eyebrow labels, large but compact headlines, and offset product frames that sit slightly beyond the text column. Landing sections alternate between a narrative rail and an instrument rail. Auth pages use a split stage, with the form anchored in a narrow panel and a branded signal field on the opposite side. The backoffice remains dense and task-oriented with a persistent sidebar, slim topbar, stacked KPI modules, and right-side drawers for context.

### Signature Elements
1. **Signal rail:** a 3px cobalt vertical rail, used beside active page titles, key landing callouts, and drawer headers.
2. **Evidence chips:** compact label/value pairs such as “Headroom · KES 11/day” or “Updated · 12 min ago” with tiny status dots.
3. **Floating instrument frames:** dashboard/table fragments shown inside off-white panes with crisp shadows and a single highlighted row or metric.

### Interaction Philosophy
Interactions should feel like operating a precise instrument: hover states are quiet lifts, buttons respond with a 0.97 press scale, and selections reveal context instead of navigating away unexpectedly. High-stakes actions require visible confirmation. Opening a product or customer reveals a right-side drawer with an anchored close path and enough evidence to act confidently.

### Animation
Use short, custom ease-out transitions (160–240ms) for buttons, nav, chips, and drawer entry. Landing signal rails and floating frames can fade/translate 10px on first view with 40ms stagger. The product-details drawer should enter from the right at 260ms with opacity and translate only; the overlay should fade at 180ms. Avoid looping motion. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` by disabling entrance transforms and leaving only instant state changes.

### Typography System
Use **DM Sans** for interface copy, labels, and data because its geometry stays readable at compact sizes. Use **Instrument Sans** for display headings to give the landing page a subtle editorial voice without becoming decorative. Headlines: 48–72px landing, 30–38px auth, 28px app page titles. Body: 15–17px landing, 13–14px app. Metadata: 11–12px uppercase with 0.12em tracking. Use weight contrast rather than excessive italics.

### Brand Essence
Onfon Next helps Onfon operators turn repayment context into responsible accessory growth, without losing the customer in the handoff. **Clear, disciplined, human.**

### Brand Voice
Headlines are direct, assured, and specific. CTAs describe the next operational move instead of using vague filler. Microcopy explains the reason, the constraint, or the consequence.

Example headline: **“Turn repayment context into the next useful thing.”**

Example CTA: **“Open the control station”**

Example microcopy: **“Offer only when the customer has room to say yes.”**

### Wordmark & Logo
The mark is a compact cobalt square containing three offset white bars: the shortest bar for the phone baseline, the middle bar for the accessory attach, and the tallest bar for the control station. The bars align on a shared bottom edge to suggest disciplined growth. The wordmark is set in a custom-feeling all-caps treatment with a small “NEXT” counter-label, never as a default logo text lockup.

### Signature Brand Color
**Onfon Blue — #175CD3.** A confident, legible cobalt that communicates permission, clarity, and forward motion without drifting into generic startup blue.

## Style Decisions

The backoffice must always read as an operations station: persistent left navigation, slim topbar, dense tables/KPI modules, and contextual detail areas take priority over spacious centered dashboard cards.

The cobalt signal rail is mandatory beside active page titles, major decision contexts, and drawer/detail headers so Onfon Blue becomes a navigational instrument, not only a CTA color.

The wordmark uses a custom-feeling all-caps ONFON treatment with a smaller NEXT counter-label; the brand is never presented as plain default text beside the mark.

## Style Decisions

Customer and Merchant pages share the same neo-Swiss utility frame, but Customers lead with repayment score, headroom, and suppression evidence while Merchants lead with stock accuracy, fulfilment, routed-order traces, and dealer health.

Every finished backoffice page must keep the ONFON NEXT mark, a persistent left navigation shell, the cobalt active-state rail, and a strict label → value → constraint → action evidence hierarchy.

All new operator workspaces use a compact station instrument strip beneath the header: policy basis, guardrail, current state, or next move appear as label → value → evidence before the primary work surface.

## Brand Reference Refresh — Accepted Direction

The supplied Onfon Next concept sheet is now the reference for the public brand system. The selected mark is an orbital, forward-moving N/path symbol in Onfon red and black; the wordmark is ONFON NEXT with a red NEXT emphasis. Keep the Quiet Operations layout and evidence hierarchy, but let Onfon red #E60012, near-black #111111, white, and soft neutral surfaces carry the public-facing brand signal. Use cobalt only where the operational interface needs a secondary system accent.

The landing message makes the architecture explicit: Onfon Mobile represents what the customer has today; Onfon Next represents what the customer needs next. Preferred positioning line: “Onfon knows what comes next.”

## Full Application Palette Refresh — Accepted Direction

The Onfon red/black identity applies across the full application, not only the landing page. Onfon red #E60012 now carries primary actions, active navigation, selected states, and key opportunity signals; near-black carries the station frame and text; white and mineral neutrals carry surfaces; gray carries routine healthy or completed states. Blue, purple, green, and amber are not used as broad brand accents. The landing hero dashboard uses the same red/black treatment so the product visual and interface chrome read as one system.
